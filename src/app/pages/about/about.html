About Me

I recently completed my PhD in Bioinformatics at the University of Lausanne (UNIL), in the CSO Lab, on large-scale 3D genome organization. My work sits at the intersection of data engineering, epigenomics, and web software development. Understanding various biological disciplines puts me in a unique position to bridge the gap between wet lab and dry lab, and to build tools that are both powerful and user-friendly for biologists, with rigorous software engineering principles.

Engineer at heart, I enjoy building both software and hardware. My PhD started in an experimental direction — designing microfluidic chips for intricate live-microscopy experiments with bacteria. The sudden passing of my first supervisor reshaped my path: I joined the Computational Systems Oncology group at my department and shifted to bioinformatics and eventually towards sofware and database engineering. What began as biological data analysis gradually grew into a deeper interest in software architecture and database systems, eventually leading to HiCverse — a full-stack platform for 3D genomics data built on Angular, FastAPI, and a Parquet-based data lake on AWS S3.

Before my PhD, I worked as a scientific assistant in the Control Theory and Systems Biology group at D-BSSE (Basel), developing optogenetic systems — an interest I first picked up as a side project during my neuroscience work at the Donders Institute in Nijmegen, where I also developed ImageJ pipelines for cortical interneuron quantification.

Outside the terminal: I have a keen interest in 3D printing and Arduino circuits. An avid advocate of open-source tools and digital autonomy — daily Emacs user, interested in self-hosted infrastructure and privacy-respecting software (Syncthing, Nextcloud, Proton ecosystem).

Based in Basel, Switzerland, but often found in the Carpathian mountains of south Poland. Open to roles in the Basel / Zurich area.
